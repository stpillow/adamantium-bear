# Program questions

Places where `docs/program.md` is ambiguous, or where it and `PROMPT.md` pull in different directions. For each one the app takes the reading that is more conservative for the knee and keeps going (working agreement 6). Nothing here changes training content; where the data model had to bend, the model bent.

Status key: **confirm** = reading taken, Shawn to confirm or overrule · **confirmed** = Shawn kept the reading · **answered** = Shawn chose; the answer follows · **open** = needs an answer · **FYI** = model decision, no answer needed. Every confirm and open item through 59 was put to Shawn on Nov 1; his answers are recorded on each line. Items 60 to 62 were put to him on Nov 2 and kept. Items 63 to 85 (equipment, M4b) were put to him on Nov 2; his answers are recorded on each line. Items from 108 on (M5 and its review) carry their status on the line.

## Found while building the week (M4)

59. **Moving a session has no written limits** (PROMPT rule 10 says only "to another day in the same week"). A move swaps two days, so moving a day that already holds a finished Session A would put A on another day and Today would offer it a second time. ## Found in the M4 review

60. **A run day with no morning check yet.** program.md swaps a run when the knee says Hold, and rule 3 and the glossary say unknown is hold, never clean. The engine treated a missing check like a clean one, so skipping the check left the tempo, strides and faster effort preset while logging a hold took them away. Reading: with no check today the suggested miles stand and no swap is suggested, but strides, the tempo and any faster effort stay off, with the line "log the morning check first; strides, the tempo and any faster effort wait on a clean morning". The other option, the full hold reading, would swap every run done before the check is logged; the hard parts are what the check exists to clear. **confirmed** Nov 2

61. **Changing the start date after sessions were moved.** PROMPT section 2 makes the start date editable in Settings and says nothing about stored moves. Moves are stored by program week, so a new start date put every move on other calendar dates, onto days already logged, which is what question 59 exists to prevent. Reading: a move stays on the calendar dates it was made for. Saving a new start date renumbers each move by the whole weeks between the two Mondays, in the same write as the date; a move that now falls before week 1 is dropped, and Settings says how many were. The phase-entered week is a program week number too and is rebased the same way, never below week 1 (`rebaseWeekNumber`); the date saves only on an explicit tap, since a picker can pass through several Mondays. **confirmed** Nov 2

62. **Logging Sunday's run on Monday.** The M4 brief limits the logger's date choice to "this program week up to today", so once Monday came a run done on Sunday (a run moved onto Sunday, or one run on the off day) could only be dated Monday. Its miles and its reading landed in the new week: Sunday read Missed, Monday's run read Done, and a hold on Sunday capped the week after next instead of the next one. Reading: the choices are this program week up to today plus, on a Monday, yesterday, the last day of the week before, so the run keeps its date and counts in the week it happened. Nothing earlier than yesterday: a finished week otherwise stays as it was logged (question 59). **confirmed** Nov 2

## The evidence layer (Batch F)

191. **The GRADE rule** (`derivedGrade` and `startProblem` in `src/schema/evidence.ts`). (a) If the source itself reports GRADE certainty for the relevant outcome, that is the start (`sourceGrade`); a range takes its lower bound (Perriman 2018 "low to moderate" is low, Filbay 2025 "low to very low" is very low, Gomes 2023 "very low to moderate" is very low); after it only indirectness applies, because the source's GRADE already counts imprecision. (b) Otherwise the start is `assessedAs`: high for an RCT (a randomized crossover or within-subject trial included: Palmieri-Smith 2013, Pancar 2026, Ribeiro 2014, and Ellestad 2024, whose carries and holds were done in a randomized order and compared within subjects) or a review of RCTs only (Kassiano 2025, Impellizzeri 2021, Lauersen 2014, McCrary 2015); low for observational, diagnostic-accuracy, cohort, case-control and cross-sectional designs and for reviews of mixed or observational studies (scoping reviews included, and a review of "randomized and/or controlled trials", such as Nascimento 2018, whose pool admits non-randomized controlled trials); very low for case series, narrative reviews, expert consensus and guidelines without their own GRADE rating. Diagnostic and reliability studies start low on purpose, stricter than GRADE's own convention for test accuracy. (c) Down one level for indirectness (population or outcome) and one for imprecision (a single small study); very low is the floor. (d) The basis is stored in words beside the grade, and the schema refuses a stored grade the rule disagrees with, both `sourceGrade` and `assessedAs` or neither, imprecision on a source's own GRADE, any start higher than the design's, and a meta-analysis or systematic review started high whose basis doesn't say it pooled randomized trials only, or admits "and/or", non-randomized, quasi-randomized, mixed, observational or cohort studies (`reviewPoolProblem`). (e) A start lower than the design's is a named exception and needs a one-sentence `startReason`; there is one: **Thomeé 1997** is an RCT, but its randomized comparison was isometric versus eccentric training with pain monitoring in both arms, so for this question it is read as a case series (very low, as the verified record grades it; under (b) alone it would be low). *Confirm*: keep the exception, or grade it low.
